What am I looking at?

Color shows the flow field you selected (vorticity = local spin of the air: red spins one way, blue the other). The dark shapes are solid bodies the air must go around. Drag coefficients are measured the honest way — by adding up the momentum the air actually hands to each body, every step.

Cd = 2Fx / (ρ U² A),   Cl = 2Fy / (ρ U² A)

F is the momentum-exchange force summed over boundary links, A the frontal size the solver actually sees (voxel-counted), U the inflow speed. While you drag a body, the readouts grey out for about one flow-through: moving an obstacle re-fills uncovered cells with equilibrium air, which fires a brief pressure pop — the frozen gauge is the honest alternative to showing you that spike.

§2

Self-checks — don't trust us, run them

The in-browser engine and the Python engine that produced every precomputed number on this page are checked against the same three golden cases, chosen so that together they exercise all the physics: pure bulk flow, solid walls, and open in/outflow with force measurement. The Python side passed these in its test suite; press the buttons to make the browser engine prove itself on identical cases, live.

Self-check theater

One button, three acts: bulk physics → walls → open tunnel + forces. Same tolerances as the cards below — presentation only, never softer.

Act —
Why these three cases, and what would failure mean?

Taylor–Green is a swirling flow with an exact pencil-and-paper answer for how fast it dies down — if collision and streaming are right, the decay rate must match to 2%. Poiseuille is flow in a pipe: the velocity profile must be an exact parabola, which tests the walls. The cylinder sheds vortices at a rhythm (the Strouhal number) that decades of experiments pin near 0.16–0.20 in this confined setup — that tests inflow, outflow, and the force measurement at once.

If a check fails here, the page says so in red and the live lab's numbers should not be trusted. That's the deal: rigor includes showing you the failure.

§3

The drafting study

The headline experiment. Two race-car profiles over a moving road; we sweep the gap between them, measure every force, then let the measured aerodynamics drive the cars' motion. The result: the trailing car reels the leader in on its own — no scripting, just the wake. Play the catch-up in the live lab →

The full story: method, equations, caveats

The trailing car hides from the wind inside the leader's wake — the same reason cyclists ride in a paceline. Air that has already been shoved aside by the leader hits the follower more gently.

Method: 2D Ahmed-body silhouettes on a 700×200 lattice, Re = 100 on car height, road and ceiling moving at the inflow speed (the physically correct frame for a car on a road — a stationary floor would grow a fake boundary layer under the cars). Each gap is a separate run; each car's drag is measured by momentum exchange on its own boundary links, mean ± std over the final 5 000 steps.

The motion coupling is quasi-static: the cars' equation of motion

m dv/dt = Fengine − ½ ρ v² A Cd(gap)

uses Cd(gap) interpolated from the measured map. That is valid only because the gap closes far more slowly than the wake refreshes — the timescale ratio is printed next to the result. This is a 2D analogue; real cars live at Reynolds numbers ~10 000× higher where wakes are turbulent and recover faster.

§4

Why birds fly in V

The aerial sibling of drafting — but fully 3D. Each wingtip sheds a trailing vortex; just outboard of the leader's wingtips the air moves upward. A trailing bird parked in that upwash gets free lift. We measure the mechanism first, then the benefit.

Method and honesty notes

A real goose formation flies at Reynolds numbers in the hundreds of thousands; our voxelized gliding bird flies at about 200. At this scale the tip vortices are fat and diffuse quickly, so the study is gated: first a single wing must show measurable lift above the force noise (it did, or you would be reading that instead); then the wake plane must show the textbook sign pattern — downwash between the tips, upwash outboard; only then do we place trailing birds at the measured upwash maximum, not the textbook wingtip offset, because at low Reynolds the vortex line drifts inboard. The assert is on the mechanism and the sign of the benefit; the magnitude at this Reynolds is reported as-is, not extrapolated to real geese.

§7

How this simulation works

Instead of solving the airflow equations directly, a lattice-Boltzmann solver plays a very fast, very simple board game. Space is a grid; on each cell live nine little packets of air, each committed to one direction (stay, the 4 compass points, the 4 diagonals). Every tick, two things happen: packets collide — each cell nudges its packets toward local equilibrium — and packets stream — each hops one cell along its direction. That's the whole game. Do it millions of times and honest-to-goodness fluid dynamics emerges: wakes, vortex streets, drafting.

The actual equations

BGK collision relaxes the populations fi toward equilibrium:

fi ← fi + (fieq − fi)/τ,   ν = (τ − ½)/3 fieq = wi ρ (1 + 3 ci·u + 4.5 (ci·u)² − 1.5 |u|²)

Walls are half-way bounce-back: a packet that would enter a solid cell is reflected at the midpoint, which is why every body is effectively one cell fatter than its mask (we use that effective size consistently in both Reynolds number and drag). Forces are momentum exchange — the exact momentum handed to the body by every reflecting packet. The browser engine runs the identical algorithm as WebGL2 fragment shaders, storing population deviations to keep fp32 precision honest.

What this is not

  • Not engineering-grade aerodynamics: real cars live at Re ~10⁷, we run Re ~10²–10³. Wakes here are laminar and recover more slowly — stated wherever it matters.
  • The live lab is 2D; the gallery and bird study are true 3D but at modest voxel resolution (stated per card).
  • Low-Mach only: inflow ≤ 0.12 lattice units; the guardrails refuse settings that would break this.
  • The ship is simulated fully immersed (wind-tunnel mode) — a free water surface is a different, much harder simulation.
  • Drafting dynamics are quasi-static (aero map + ODE), valid because the printed timescale ratio ≫ 1; cars do not move within a lattice run.
